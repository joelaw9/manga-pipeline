# manga_pipeline spec — Junk decisions

Part of the spec; the index is [`SPEC.md`](../../SPEC.md). Update this file in the same change as the behaviour it describes.

## Junk decisions

Junk is what a scanlation group or an aggregator added to a chapter: credits, recruitment, donation and Discord notices, site promos and ads, anti-piracy notices. A scanlation group's logo badge stamped on a page is junk too: it is blanked, not dropped. Official content stays: chapter title cards, publisher, copyright and localization credits, end cards, author notes, content warnings. The junk stage (`process`) finds the units of a chapter that may be junk, applies the stored decisions, and holds the chapter while a unit is undecided. Claude makes the decisions the store lacks. A decision never rewrites a chapter already past junk (pages are never re-encoded).

### Units

A chapter's layout is its series' (`strip` or `pages`, SPEC "State"). Cover-named pages are never junk-checked or reshaped.

- Strip: the chapter's non-cover pages stacked at their modal width. Its units lie in the first and last 6000 px: an edge block is the content between 2 gaps (≥ 12 blank rows at 1000 px wide), at least 40 px tall and not of 1 colour; a probe is the piece of an edge block from the chapter edge to one of its hard joins (a seam where unrelated images were glued with no gap); a tail piece is a gap-free run after the chapter's credit start (the first removed tail range that is not a watermark). A removed tail range is a watermark when its decision removed 2 or more tail ranges of the chapter, when its key matches a middle block of the chapter, or when its decision's key matches one the series' earlier chapters showed to be a watermark: the keys of the decisions that acted as watermarks are remembered per series (with the chapter's junk outcome, whichever it is; knowledge: `state reset` keeps them). Tail pieces that are blank, of 1 colour, or a sliver under 130 px between 2 credit ranges are removed without a decision.
- Pages: the edge pages (the first 2 and last 3 non-cover pages). An edge page is 1 unit with 2 keys: its whole page, and its template frame (the page's outer border at 48 × 72) when that border carries content. Page decisions on the first and frame decisions on the second both apply, and the one ranking first decides; as a candidate the page is offered by its frame when it has one, else by the whole page. A page taller than the series' page shape also gives a band unit at each edge where a credit band sits on it (cut at a gutter, or at a seam that is no art). A stitched chapter (median page shape ≥ 1.8 × the series') gets no page units: it is only split back into pages. Uniform white pages at either end of the remaining pages are dropped without a decision, unless that would drop every page.
- Logos (both layouts; not in a stitched chapter): on each non-cover page that is gray but for 1 compact coloured component (page class gray-mark, `images.classify`), the badge: its tight box (the pixels whose channel spread is > 40, sparse rows and columns trimmed), its padded box (the tight box grown by 0.75 × its height, clamped to the page: what a blank overwrites), and the median colour of the 10 px ring around the padded box (the fill). A logo is blankable when at least 95 % of its ring lies within 10 of the fill on every channel; any other logo is no unit (it only goes gray at convert). A page has at most 1 logo.

Each unit has a key: its perceptual signature (1 64-bit pHash per roughly square tile, plus its aspect), for a frame its border vector and aspect, for a logo its template (the tight box's pixels and the mask of its coloured pixels). The match rules and constants are those the stored decisions were made with and are fixed: signatures match when they have the same tile count, every tile within Hamming 6 and the aspects within 3 %; frames when their RMS distance is ≤ 18 and the aspects within 12 %; a logo matches a stored template when their aspects are within 10 %, their masks overlap with IoU ≥ 0.7 (the logo resized to the template) and the mean channel difference over the template's coloured pixels is ≤ 40. Units of 1 colour have no key and are never junk. A signature whose every tile is a uniform image's is unsignable: it would match any blank or flat rows. A unit with one matches no decision, is never offered (a block's unsignable probe is passed over for its next) and is never the middle block a watermark matches; no decision is written on one, and the imports leave such rows out. An unsignable band is no band: its page is judged as a page. Logo matching is not transitive, so a chapter's logos are grouped into variants (a logo joins every group holding a match) and a variant is decided as a whole by its representative templates (a subset every member matches): a keep on any of them wins, else a junk on any.

### Decisions

A decision is a key, a unit kind, a verdict (`junk` or `keep`), a scope (1 series, or global), who made it (`import`, `claude`, `human`) and when. The series of a decision is its series' rules key (SPEC "Series identification"), as are those of sightings, candidates and watermarks; an imported decision whose folder name is no series' rules key yet applies once it is one. A unit is decided by the decisions of the kinds that act on it (block and probe decisions on edge blocks and probes, piece on tail pieces, page on pages, band on bands, frame on frames, logo on logos), in this order: decisions the pipeline made (`claude`, `human`) first, so one wins for every signature it covers, their keeps before their junk; then the imported ones: the series' keeps, the series' junk, global keeps, global junk; newest first within each (keeps go first because matching is not transitive: a unit that matches both a kept key and a junk key stays kept, whichever is newer); the first that matches decides. A `junk` strip unit's rows are removed, a `junk` page dropped, a `junk` band cropped off its page, a `junk` logo blanked (its padded box filled with the fill); `keep` leaves it. A removal never leaves more than 1 gutter where it joins the strip, and never leaves a chapter of blank rows only or under 1500 px when it was longer: such a chapter is `needs_human`. Every new decision is series-scoped; global decisions come only from the import.

### Candidates

An undecided unit becomes a candidate when, with the chapter in hand:

- it is near an approved signature (same tile count, every tile within Hamming 8, aspect within 10 %; block, band and probe decisions of any series or global; strip units);
- its OCR text has a keyword hit (strip edge blocks and probes; `junk.keywords`, case-insensitive substrings; a `junk.weak` term counts only on a unit at most 800 px tall; a `junk.paired` term only beside another keyword; any `junk.suppress` term cancels every hit; OCR text that holds a NUL character cannot be stored: its chapter is `needs_human` at once, with the unit's rows named);
- it matches units of at least 3 chapter identities of the series, counted over every scan the pipeline has made of the series and the bulk converter's sightings of edge blocks, pages and bands, which `junk import` brings over (each chapter by the identity of its archive name);
- its chapter is among the series' first 3 chapter identities by first sighting, then by season and chapter number (the time a chapter identity was first seen is knowledge: it survives `state reset`; every imported sighting counts as seen at the same time, before any scan): every keyed unmatched edge block;
- it is the rest of an edge block whose edge piece a junk decision removed (the edge piece is often only part of a credit block);
- it is a tail piece the rules above did not remove;
- it is a band no decision covers, on any page of a page-format chapter;
- it is a page-format edge page whose size no middle page of the chapter has, the chapter's last page when it is mostly plain with a little colour in a gray chapter (a credits page), or an edge band page whose bands no decision covers;
- it is a blankable logo whose tight box survives the rest of the edit (its page not dropped, part of the box inside the page's kept band crop, not all of its rows removed from the strip): every such logo of an undecided variant, 1 candidate per variant, keyed by the first of its representative templates that an open candidate matches, else its first.

Every ready junk-stage chapter of a series is scanned before any of them is decided, so what repeats across them is counted first. A chapter's scan is kept while its series and its content digest are the chapter's; a chapter moved to another series (`series link`) or holding another download is scanned again, and so is a chapter whose stored scan predates logos (it holds none).

1 candidate per key: a unit joins the series' candidate of its kind whose key it matches, else it opens one; the same credit page in 20 chapters is 1 candidate. A candidate is open while a chapter at the junk stage that is not done has a unit matching it and no decision covers it; a decision from any source closes it. A unit that matches only a closed candidate opens its own (matching is not transitive). Each open candidate's images sit in `paths.candidates` (on the share, so the machine that runs `junk review` sees what any machine found): the unit (at most 1000 px wide; for a logo, its surroundings before and after the blank, side by side), the unit in its context (300 px of strip above and below, the neighbouring pages, or a logo's page), its OCR text, why it is a candidate and how many chapters show it. A new candidate's folder is named by its series key, kind and key (hashed) and by the chapter and claim that wrote it, and the candidate row stores that name, so a folder is never shared by 2 rows. A machine writes the images only while it still holds the chapter's claim (SPEC "State").

`junk review` cleans up, at its start, in 1 transaction: a candidate it finds not open is marked closed (when not marked yet), one it finds open again loses its mark, and a candidate marked closed more than 1 hour ago is deleted; a candidate listed by a review that has not ended is never marked or deleted. Then it removes the folders of the rows it deleted, and a folder no row names once it is a day old (another machine may have written it and not yet recorded its row). A chapter that joins a candidate whose row was deleted fails, and its retry writes the images anew.

A chapter with an open candidate waits on `junk_decision`. `process` (on any machine) ends the wait as soon as 1 of its candidates is covered by a decision or gone, before the unsure and stuck test (Review); it then decides the chapter again from its stored scan and waits on what is still undecided (a decision can make another candidate moot: a dropped page's band).

### Review

`junk review` runs on the LXC only, once in the whole fleet at a time (SPEC "Run lock"). It runs `claude -p` on the same machine when some open candidate can be listed, and exits at once otherwise. A systemd timer starts it; no part starts or waits for Claude. It hands `claude -p` the Claude token from the config (a secret, like the Kavita key) and no other credential: Claude's environment holds no `MANGA_PIPELINE_` variable and no API key, and its `junk` commands read a copy of the run's config with every secret set to null. So they reach the database only over the server's local socket as the machine user, which needs no password (SPEC "Config"): the review runs on the machine that holds the server. The token is never written to a log or an error message. Claude may read the candidates folder and run `junk list`, `junk show` and `junk decide`, nothing else (`junk list --all` is refused inside a review); it lists the candidates, looks at the images, and records each verdict: junk, keep or unsure. The pipeline reads decisions only from the store.

A review counts for each candidate it listed, once the review ends. A candidate listed in `junk.review_limit` reviews and still undecided is stuck: it is never listed to Claude again. Each run records its start, end, result and last success in the database, like a part. A chapter with an unsure or stuck candidate is `needs_human` and keeps the candidates it waits on open; a person decides the candidate with `junk decide` (`junk list --all` lists every open candidate) and clears the chapter with `retry`.

### Import

`junk import PATH` copies decisions from the bulk converter's `junk.sqlite3`, and `junk import --database NAME` from another pipeline database on the same server (logged in to as the pipeline's own), without writing to either. Active decisions of the kinds above come over with their scope; the bulk converter's approve is `junk`, its keep `keep`; a block or probe approve that carries a chain comes over as a plain decision on its key, without the chain; a logo mark comes over as a logo decision on its template (the bulk converter records approvals globally and keeps for a series); cut rows and chains are not imported (the command reports their count). From a bulk-converter store it also brings the sightings of edge blocks, pages and bands (recurrence), as seen before any pipeline scan. An import rebuilds the imported rows: in 1 transaction it deletes every imported decision (and, from a bulk-converter store, every imported sighting) and loads the source's rows fresh, so a decision the bulk converter deleted or changed is followed exactly; running it again with no change in the source changes nothing. The pipeline's own decisions (`claude`, `human`) and sightings are never touched, and a source row on a key the pipeline decided is left out, as is one on an unsignable key (the command counts them). From another pipeline database (cutover), its `claude` / `human` decisions arrive as such and its imported ones as imported; its sightings are not read. Nothing refers to a decision's row id, so a rebuild breaks no link. The decisions in a bulk-converter series record (schema 4) are imported the same way when the series' baseline is taken; a record whose decision rows are malformed does not parse (its series' chapters wait on `bulk_conversion`). A schema-3 record carries none: its decisions are in the store. A database whose baselines were taken before it imported record rows gets the same decisions from `junk import` of the store, which holds every decision a record carries.

### The chapter edit

A decided chapter's edit is 1 JSON object, read by convert. It always holds all 6 keys; a key that does not apply to the chapter's layout holds its empty value. Page indexes are integers: the download's pages in archive order (`archive.archive_pages`); a cover-named page is never in any of them.

| Key | Type | Empty | Layout | Holds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `drop_pages` | list of int | `[]` | pages | the pages dropped: junk pages and frames, white end pages |
| `bands` | list of `{"page": int, "box": [int, int, int, int]}` | `[]` | pages | a page cropped by its junk bands: `box` is the part kept, `[left, top, right, bottom]` (right and bottom exclusive) in the pixels of the page as decoded (`images.open_flat`) |
| `join` | list of `[int, int]` | `[]` | pages | `[page, sliver]`: a sliver is a page cut off the bottom of the page before it (same width, at most half its height, the seam continuous); rejoined under it. A page in 3 pieces is `[[a, b], [b, c]]` |
| `split` | bool | `false` | pages | true when the chapter is stitched (whole pages joined into tall images): convert cuts it back into pages; a split chapter has no drops, bands or joins |
| `blank` | list of `{"page": int, "box": [int, int, int, int], "fill": [int, int, int]}` | `[]` | both | a junk logo: `box` is its padded box, `[left, top, right, bottom]` (right and bottom exclusive) in the pixels of the source page before any crop, `fill` its ring's median colour (0–255); at most 1 per page, never on a dropped or cover-named page |
| `strip_removed` | list of `{"start": int, "end": int, "why": str}` | `[]` | strip | rows removed, `end` exclusive, in the stack of the non-cover pages at their modal width; final (the gutter rule and the minimum length applied). `why`: `match` (a junk decision on an edge block, probe or remainder), `piece` (a junk decision on a tail piece), `blank` or `wedge` (removed by rule after the credit start), `join` (blank rows the gutter rule removes) |

Convert fills blanks first, on the source pages (only for a chapter it encodes to AVIF), then drops pages, then crops bands, then joins; joins are found among the kept pages, cropped where cropped. Convert treats a chapter with no edit, or a malformed one, as `needs_human` (Phase 6's rule, SPEC "Chapter stages"). Malformed includes, beyond a wrong key or type: a page index the chapter lacks, a page dropped twice, a drop or a band on a cover-named page, 2 bands on 1 page, a band on a dropped page, a band `box` that is empty or not inside its page, a `strip_removed` entry with other keys than `start`, `end` and `why`, with rows that are not `0 <= start < end`, or ending past the strip's last row.
