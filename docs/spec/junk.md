# manga_pipeline spec — Junk decisions

Part of the spec; the index is [`SPEC.md`](../../SPEC.md). Update this file in the same change as the behaviour it describes.

## Junk decisions

Junk is what a scanlation group, an uploader or an aggregator added to a chapter, and promotion that is not part of it; each of these is junk as a whole unit, its own official art, logos and text included: credits; recruitment, donation and Discord notices; uploader notes and filler images; a scanlator credit page decorated with art (an illustration, a reused story panel, cover art), even when it credits the artist's social account; a scanlator card mostly covering an official credit line; site promos, aggregator watermark lines on a plain background and comment-section screenshots; scanlator translator's notes; next-chapter previews, official or not; ads, official ones too (platform and publisher banners and ads, series banners with a URL, preorder, "on sale" and spinoff cards, platform notices); a ripper's header banner at a chapter's top (official art, not a story panel, with the official platform's URL across it); an anti-piracy card or band holding nothing but the notice and a platform or publisher logo or URL, not part of an official end card. A scanlation group's or an aggregator site's logo badge stamped on a page is junk too: it is blanked, not dropped, unless it is part of the art or blanking it loses anything else. Official content stays: story panels, chapter title cards, end cards, publisher, copyright, localization and staff credits, publisher marks, logos and watermarks, author notes and social links, content warnings. A publisher's or platform's own mark, logo or watermark that belongs to no junk item and is obtrusive or covers the art makes its unit unsure; otherwise a unit holding content that belongs to no junk item is kept (an official end card that carries an anti-piracy notice (a cut decision may remove only its notice lines); a promo, watermark or TL note on a story panel). The junk stage (`process`) finds the units of a chapter that may be junk, applies the stored decisions, and holds the chapter while a unit is undecided. Claude makes the decisions the store lacks. A decision never rewrites a chapter already past junk (pages are never re-encoded).

### Units

A chapter's layout is its series' (`strip` or `pages`, SPEC "State"). Cover-named pages are never junk-checked or reshaped.

- Strip: the chapter's non-cover pages stacked at their modal width. Its units lie in the first and last 6000 px: an edge block is the content between 2 gaps (≥ 12 blank rows at 1000 px wide), at least 40 px tall and not of 1 colour; a probe is the piece of an edge block from the chapter edge to one of its hard joins (a seam where unrelated images were glued with no gap); a tail piece is a gap-free run after the chapter's credit start (the first tail band that is not a watermark; a tail band is a tail range a block or probe decision removed, or a cut decision with credit; a card range or a cut without credit is none), outside the rows a matched card keeps. A tail band is a watermark when its decision removed 2 or more tail bands of the chapter (1 chain match counts once), or, for a block or probe decision, when its key matches a middle block of the chapter or one the series' earlier chapters showed to be a watermark (a cut decision's key is never compared, nor remembered): the keys of the decisions that acted as watermarks are remembered per series (with the chapter's junk outcome, whichever it is; knowledge: `state reset` keeps them). Tail pieces that are blank, of 1 colour, or a sliver under 130 px between 2 credit ranges are removed without a decision; rows a matched card keeps are never removed by these rules. Rest of card (W5): after the tail pass and the late cuts, a chapter's tail pieces in gaps between the ranges steps 1-3 removed (block, probe and remainder matches, cards and cuts) are its bundle, decided or not, except in a gap that holds a card hole or mixes pieces an unmarked piece keep matches with pieces none matches (each piece there stands alone); a piece the late cuts wholly cover leaves it. The bundle's undecided members are ordinary piece candidates; the scan record keeps the bundle's candidate ids and its member count (decided members and members sharing a candidate counted), written with the candidates it waits on and reset with them by a new scan. A bundle whose members' decisions differ holds nothing and gives the chapter the note `rest of card mixed: rows A-B[, C-D]` (every member's rows), replacing its earlier one at each Done.
- Chains and cuts: a block or probe junk decision may carry its card's extent, a chain: 1 or 2 sides (above, below), each 1 or more versions, each a list of segments outward from the unit (`cut` or `keep` of a height in strip widths with tile signatures, optionally exact: exactly that many rows; `edge`: to the strip end). A cut decision is a chain of 1 side whose key is its first cut segment; it removes junk no unit offers: a header glued to a panel, notice lines inside an end card, notice runs under 40 px, notes above a removed tail band. A strip is decided in this order: block and probe decisions on the edge blocks (and their probes and remainders); then, until nothing more is added, each removed unit's chain at that unit's edges and every cut decision of the series at every seed (a strip end, a removed range's edge in an edge region, a gap's edge in an edge region, the far end of a match); the tail pass; the cut decisions again at the tail pass's removals, a tail piece wholly covered then being no candidate; the candidates (no edge block inside a matched span is one); the join rule and the kept check. A version matches when each segment takes the next span (its height within 2 px or 3 %, every tile within Hamming 6 of the span's, the same tile count, no aspect test; an exact segment takes exactly its rows); versions are tried longest first, then oldest. A matched cut segment grows over a neighbouring span its ink reaches into, never past a strip end, a removed range or a keep, and never by more than its own height. A unit's chain that should match and does not (its tiles differ, it runs off the strip, or it grows past its height) and a cut decision that grows past its height hold the chapter for a person (`needs_human`, naming the decision and the rows: fix or undo the decision); a cut decision that matches nowhere is no fault. The pages are read for this only when a chain or a cut decision is tried.
- Pages: the edge pages (the first 2 and last 3 non-cover pages). An edge page is 1 unit with 2 keys: its whole page, and its template frame (the page's outer border at 48 × 72) when that border carries content. Page decisions on the first and frame decisions on the second both apply, and the one ranking first decides; as a candidate the page is offered by its frame when it has one, else by the whole page. A page taller than the series' page shape also gives a band unit at each edge where a credit band sits on it (cut at a gutter, or at a seam that is no art). A stitched chapter (median page shape ≥ 1.8 × the series') or a re-sliced one gets no page units: it is only split back into pages. A page chapter is re-sliced when, by its sizes, it has at least 3 content pages, all 1 width, it is not stitched, fewer than a quarter of them are slivers of the page before (slice repair's), and some aspect candidate gives a cut: under it fewer than half the content pages lie within 0.15 of a whole number (1 or more) of pages, the stacked height is within 0.03 page of n ≥ 2 pages, and with the stack cut into n equal pages at least 0.15 of the inner page boundaries fall on a cut (within 0.3 % of a page, at least 2 px); of those candidates the one whose cuts the most boundaries fall on gives n (a tie: the first). With no candidate it is not re-sliced. Then, by its pixels, at 400 px width: of the cuts that cross a page's interior (at least 6 rows from its edges), at least 0.15 lie on a page boundary (the seam score of the 6 rows each side at least the slice repair's 3.5, or both sides blank); and of the boundaries between 2 pages that fall inside 1 of its pages (not on a cut), at least half are continuous (seam score at most 3.5). A chapter with no inside cut is judged by its joins alone, one with no join by its cuts alone. Its scan records n (none for a stitched chapter). Uniform white pages at either end of the remaining pages are dropped without a decision, unless that would drop every page.
- Logos (both layouts; not in a stitched or re-sliced chapter): on each non-cover page that is gray but for 1 compact coloured component (page class gray-mark, `images.classify`), the badge: its tight box (the pixels whose channel spread is > 40, sparse rows and columns trimmed), its padded box (the tight box grown by 0.75 × its height, clamped to the page: what a blank overwrites), and the median colour of the 10 px ring around the padded box (the fill). A logo is blankable when at least 95 % of its ring lies within 10 of the fill on every channel; any other logo is no unit (it only goes gray at convert). A page has at most 1 logo.

Each unit has a key: its perceptual signature (1 64-bit pHash per roughly square tile, plus its aspect), for a frame its border vector and aspect, for a logo its template (the tight box's pixels and the mask of its coloured pixels). The match rules and constants are those the stored decisions were made with and are fixed: signatures match when they have the same tile count, every tile within Hamming 6 and the aspects within 3 %; frames when their RMS distance is ≤ 18 and the aspects within 12 %; a logo matches a stored template when their aspects are within 10 %, their masks overlap with IoU ≥ 0.7 (the logo resized to the template) and the mean channel difference over the template's coloured pixels is ≤ 40. Units of 1 colour have no key and are never junk. A signature whose every tile is a uniform image's is unsignable: it would match any blank or flat rows. A unit with one matches no decision, is never offered (a block's unsignable probe is passed over for its next) and is never the middle block a watermark matches; no decision is written on one, and the imports leave such rows out. An unsignable band is no band: its page is judged as a page. Logo matching is not transitive, so a chapter's logos are grouped into variants (a logo joins every group holding a match) and a variant is decided as a whole by its representative templates (a subset every member matches): a keep on any of them wins, else a junk on any.

### Decisions

A decision is a key, a unit kind, a verdict (`junk` or `keep`), a scope (1 series, or global), who made it (`import`, `claude`, `human`) and when. A decision is active, or inactive: superseded by a newer imported decision on its key, or retired by an act of `junk redecide` (Decision commands); only active decisions decide units. A decision is never deleted: its history stays. The series of a decision is its series' rules key (SPEC "Series identification"), as are those of sightings, candidates and watermarks; an imported decision whose folder name is no series' rules key yet applies once it is one. A unit is decided by the decisions of the kinds that act on it (block and probe decisions on edge blocks and probes, piece on tail pieces, page on pages, band on bands, frame on frames, logo on logos), in this order: the series' keeps, the series' junk, global keeps, the agreed keeps of other series, their agreed junk, global junk; within each, the pipeline's decisions (`claude`, `human`) before the imported ones, then newest first (keeps go first because matching is not transitive: a unit that matches both a kept key and a junk key stays kept, whichever is newer and whoever made it); the first that matches decides. Other series agree on a unit when their active decisions (any decider, none global) of the kinds that act on it and that match it come from 2 or more series and all hold 1 verdict; 1 decision of the other verdict among them means no agreement; a decision under a series key no series holds counts as another series'. A tail piece is never decided by agreement, and a frame or a logo only to `junk`. A decision acting by agreement acts without its chain: its card is not removed and its chain never holds a chapter. No row is written, and no watermark is remembered from a unit an agreement removed: a decision made, re-decided or undone in any series can end an agreement or start one, for the chapters decided after it. When a decide ends a chapter's junk stage (done, `needs_human` or dropped from a decide), 1 log line names each unit an agreement removed or kept in its result, the unit's kind and verdict, and the decisions that agreed (for an edit refused, each agreed junk the decide looked up). A `junk` strip unit's rows are removed, a `junk` page dropped, a `junk` band cropped off its page, a `junk` logo blanked (its padded box filled with the fill); `keep` leaves it. A removal never leaves more than 1 gutter where it joins the strip, and never leaves a chapter of blank rows only or under 1500 px when it was longer: such a chapter is `needs_human`. Every new decision is series-scoped; global decisions come only from the import. A series block or probe junk decision may carry its card's extent (a chain), and a cut decision (kind `cut`: series-scoped, junk, its key the first cut segment of its chain as a signature, `tiles|h`) carries a chain of 1 side and may carry credit; a chain is canonical JSON on its decision, checked on every write by the bulk converter's row-shape rules (a cut chain's versions name their source, a unit's do not (a held row's source is its archive and the archive's sha256, one `junk cut` makes names the download's file and its pages digest); `edge` on a unit's chain only), and refused while it holds a `none` exemption or a `skip` segment (no command makes them); no other decision carries a chain or credit. Decisions are changed with `junk redecide` and `junk undo` (Decision commands); a changed decision acts on chapters decided after it (SPEC "The chapter edit"). A decision may carry the bundle mark: `junk decide` (`--keep-rest` too) sets it on a decision written on a candidate that a waiting junk-stage chapter's record lists in a bundle of more than 1 member, `junk redecide` carries it from the decision it replaces, and the import sets it on a record row whose review id is a bundle's (`rn…`); a marked keep never protects a rest-of-card gap.

### Candidates

An undecided unit becomes a candidate when, with the chapter in hand:

- it is near an approved signature (same tile count, aspect within 10 %, every tile within Hamming 8 of a decision of its series or a global one, 6 of another series' decision, and 4 when the unit is a probe whatever the decision's scope; block, band and probe junk decisions; strip units; none when such a junk decision of its series or a global one matches it at the live rule);
- its OCR text has a keyword hit (strip edge blocks and probes; `junk.keywords`, case-insensitive substrings; a `junk.weak` term counts only on a unit at most 800 px tall; a `junk.paired` term only beside another keyword; any `junk.suppress` term cancels every hit; OCR text that holds a NUL character cannot be stored: its chapter is `needs_human` at once, with the unit's rows named);
- it matches units of at least 3 chapter identities of the series, counted over every scan the pipeline has made of the series and the bulk converter's sightings of edge blocks, pages and bands, which the database holds from the bulk converter (imported once; none are added) (each chapter by the identity of its archive name);
- it is the rest of an edge block whose edge piece a junk decision removed (the edge piece is often only part of a credit block);
- it is a tail piece the rules above did not remove;
- it is a band no decision covers, on any page of a page-format chapter;
- it is a page-format edge page whose size no middle page of the chapter has, the chapter's last page when it is mostly plain with a little colour in a gray chapter (a credits page), or an edge band page whose bands no decision covers;
- it is a blankable logo whose tight box survives the rest of the edit (its page not dropped, part of the box inside the page's kept band crop, not all of its rows removed from the strip): every such logo of an undecided variant, 1 candidate per variant, keyed by the first of its representative templates that an open candidate matches, else its first.

A chapter's units are read by `junk.ocr_workers` OCR engines at once, each unit by 1 engine; a unit's text is the same as 1 engine reads it.

Every ready junk-stage chapter of a series is scanned before any of them is decided, so what repeats across them is counted first; a download does not wait for its series' adopted files' conversions or rebuilds to be scanned (SPEC "State"), and those conversions and rebuilds wait until every ready junk-stage chapter of the series is scanned. A chapter's scan is kept while its series and its content digest are the chapter's; a chapter moved to another series (`series link`) or holding another download is scanned again, and so is a chapter whose stored scan predates logos (it holds none) or was made under another layout than the series' now has (SPEC "State").

1 candidate per key: a unit joins the series' candidate of its kind whose key it matches, else it opens one; the same credit page in 20 chapters is 1 candidate. A candidate is open while a chapter at the junk stage that is not done has a unit matching it and no decision covers it; a decision from any source, or an agreement of other series, closes it. A unit that matches only a closed candidate opens its own (matching is not transitive). Each open candidate's images sit in `paths.candidates` (on the share, so the machine that runs `junk review` sees what any machine found): the unit (at most 1000 px wide; for a logo, its surroundings before and after the blank, side by side), the unit in its context: for a strip unit 700 rows of strip above and below, every removed range washed grey and labelled with its rows and its decision (or its rule), and a red bar beside the unit's rows, drawn at the size the reader is sent (at most 1000 px wide, 1568 px long and 1.15 megapixels); for a page unit the page and its neighbours, the unit's page or band boxed red; for a logo its page. Beside them: its OCR text, why it is a candidate and how many chapters show it, and where its opening occurrence sits (the chapter; for a strip unit its edge, its rows and the strip's height; for a page unit its page index, and a band's edge and box; for a logo its page index and box, and its rows in a strip), the removed ranges its context shows, and the decisions its reason cites. A new candidate's folder is named by its series key, kind and key (hashed) and by the chapter and claim that wrote it, and the candidate row stores that name, so a folder is never shared by 2 rows. A machine writes the images only while it still holds the chapter's claim (SPEC "State").

`junk review` cleans up, at its start, in 1 transaction: a candidate it finds not open is marked closed (when not marked yet), one it finds open again loses its mark, and a candidate marked closed more than 1 hour ago is deleted; a candidate listed by a review that has not ended is never marked or deleted. Then it moves every folder a decision names and no candidate row does into `<paths.candidates>/decided/` (the decision's images, kept as long as the decision), removes the folders of the rows it deleted, and a folder no candidate names once it is a day old (another machine may have written it and not yet recorded its row); `decided` is never swept. A chapter that joins a candidate whose row was deleted fails, and its retry writes the images anew.

A chapter with an open candidate waits on `junk_decision`. `process` (on any machine) ends the wait as soon as 1 of its candidates is covered by a decision or gone, before the unsure and stuck test (Review); it then decides the chapter again from its stored scan (reading the download's pages only when a chain or a cut decision is tried) and waits on what is still undecided (a decision can make another candidate moot: a dropped page's band).

### Review

`junk review` runs on the LXC only, once in the whole fleet at a time (SPEC "Run lock"). `junk list` lists the candidates a series at a time: series in the order of their first candidate (fewest reviews first; among those, the candidates shown by a waiting chapter that does not replace an adopted file and is no rebuild (SPEC "State") first, then the oldest), each series' listable candidates together, until `junk.review_batch`; a series larger than the batch is cut to the room left, never inside a bundle (a candidate comes with the other listable ids of every bundle it is in, those bundles' own ids, no further): the series is cut before a set that does not fit the room left, which waits for a later review, unless nothing is listed yet and that set alone exceeds the batch (then it is listed whole and the listing stops); one that does not fit the room left waits for a later review while the listing goes on, until the batch is full. `junk list` (in a review, with `--all` and without) labels the candidates of every bundle of more than 1 member that waiting chapters record `bundle N` (chapters' identical id sets are 1 bundle; a candidate in several bundles carries each label), apart from near groups; a bundle whose other members are unsure, stuck or past the batch is shown in part. Inside a review a line per bundle names its listed ids and its sheets, written with the groups' sheets; the review judges a bundle's members together, and a bundle mixing junk and content `unsure`. Inside a review, candidates of 1 series and 1 signature kind whose keys are near (same tile count, every tile within Hamming 10, aspect within 10 %, linked in chains) form a group; its line names its ids and its sheets: grids of their units, each labelled with its id, no sheet over 1,568 px on its long edge or 1.15 megapixels, written for that review and removed when it ends. A review lists once: a later `junk list` under its id gives what it listed that is still open and not unsure, and adds nothing. It runs `claude -p` on the same machine when some open candidate can be listed, and exits at once otherwise. A systemd timer starts it; no part starts or waits for Claude. It hands `claude -p` the Claude token from the config (a secret, like the Kavita key) and no other credential: Claude's environment holds no `MANGA_PIPELINE_` variable and no API key, and its `junk` commands read a copy of the run's config with every secret set to null. So they reach the database only through a login the server trusts from its own machine without a password (SPEC "Config"): the review runs on the machine that holds the server. The token is never written to a log or an error message. Claude may read the candidates folder and run `junk list`, `junk show`, `junk decide` and `junk cut`, nothing else (`junk list --all` is refused inside a review). Inside a review, `junk show` and `junk decide` take only a candidate that review listed, and `junk cut` only a chapter of a series with a candidate that review listed and still holds. It lists the candidates, looks at the images, and records each verdict: junk, keep or unsure. A block or probe candidate of a strip whose unit holds both junk and content in rows of their own is cut instead: once the series' other listed candidates are decided, Claude draws its span sheets (`junk cut --spans ID`), proposes a cut of the junk rows, records it when the proposal prints a digest, and then keeps the candidate; a proposal refused twice, or one with a miss, makes the candidate unsure. A bundle mixing junk and content stays unsure (its pieces have no span sheet of their own). `junk show ID` prints the candidate's kind, series, reason, OCR text, position, the removed ranges near it, the decisions its reason cites (id, series, verdict, decider, whether it was retired or superseded, kept images), the chapters waiting on it (at the junk stage, not done; with the rows of its matching units for a strip block, probe or piece), how many chapters it was seen in with their ids (the newest of each identity with a download and a content digest; not recorded for a probe, piece or logo), and its image paths; each list longer than 10 shows its first and last 5 and how many are left out; a candidate written before its folder recorded a position prints `position: not recorded`. `junk show` and `junk decide` take 1 or more candidate ids; `junk decide` records its verdict on every id in 1 transaction and refuses them all, writing nothing, when it refuses one. Inside a review, `junk decide ID… junk --keep-rest` also keeps, in that transaction, every other candidate of that series the review listed that is still open and not unsure; it is refused for ids of 2 series, with another verdict, and outside a review. A decision made from a candidate keeps the candidate's images. `junk decide` of a candidate a decision covers names that decision, or the decisions whose agreement covers it. `junk decide ID… junk` (with `--keep-rest` too) on a frame candidate prints, for each, the page keeps of its series that keep an edge page of a stored page scan whose frame the candidate's key matches (the page keep ranks first there; `junk redecide KEEP junk` lifts it). The pipeline reads decisions only from the store.

A review counts for each candidate it listed, once the review ends, whatever its result. A review in which the permission rules refused a tool call of Claude's (a call that is no read in the candidates folder and none of the prompt's `junk` commands, such as one built with a shell variable; the prompt asks for 1 whole command per call) ends as an error; it still counts, so a candidate that keeps leading Claude into a refused call ends stuck. A candidate listed in `junk.review_limit` reviews and still undecided is stuck: it is never listed to Claude again. Each run records its start, end, result and last success in the database, like a part. Its result names the turns, the seconds, the tokens, the context of its first call and the largest context of 1 call (a call's context: its input, cache creation and cache read tokens), and the images Claude read and the candidates its `show` calls named (each tool use once, each candidate once, a run cut short included); its log line adds how many candidates it counted and how many of them Claude marked `unsure` (a mark a later decision in the same review covers still counts: the measure is how often Claude was unsure). A chapter with an unsure or stuck candidate is `needs_human` and keeps the candidates it waits on open; a person decides the candidate with `junk decide` (`junk list --all` lists every open candidate) and clears the chapter with `retry`. For a held candidate of a bundle of more than 1 member the reason names the bundle's candidate ids: `rest of card IDS: judge them together (junk decide IDS junk|keep), or for junk among content junk cut CHAPTER:A:B --record DIGEST and keep the content members`. `status` reports the junk refill: over the chapters whose scan was first decided in the last 7 days, the candidates that first decision waited on per chapter, apart for chapters whose series had no decision of its own at that moment and the others; the open candidates; the decisions Claude made per day; and the junk chapters that are `needs_human`. The aim is that candidates per chapter, then `needs_human`, fall to 0.

### Import

The bulk converter's decisions and sightings were imported once and stay in the database as knowledge; nothing imports them again, so a newly built database starts without them. The decisions in a bulk-converter series record (schema 4) are imported when the series' baseline is taken: its active decision rows of the kinds above come over with their scope; the bulk converter's approve is `junk`, its keep `keep`; a block or probe approve that carries a chain comes over as a plain decision on its key, without the chain; a logo mark comes over as a logo decision on its template (the bulk converter records approvals globally and keeps for a series); cut rows and chains are not imported. Of several rows on 1 key, the one the bulk converter obeyed wins: a keep before a junk, then the newest. A row on an unsignable key is left out (logged). An imported decision never overwrites the pipeline's own (`claude`, `human`), nor a key whose newest decision an act wrote or retired; a pipeline decision is written on a key with no active decision, and a newer imported decision is never undone by an older record row. A record whose decision rows are malformed does not parse (SPEC "State"). A schema-3 record carries none.

### Decision commands

`junk rows [SERIES]` lists the decisions of a series (its own and the global ones) or, without one, every decision, oldest first, active and not: id, series, kind, verdict, decider, time, source, state (active, superseded by N, retired by act N), the acts that changed it, whether images are kept, and whether it carries a chain and credit. `junk row ROW…` prints each decision and its kept images; an active frame junk decision also lists the page keeps that keep an edge page of a stored page scan whose frame it matches: a series decision's in its series, a global one's in every series, each named with its series, chapter and page; `--where` lists the chapter identities whose sightings hold a unit it decides now (edge blocks, edge pages, frames, bands; sightings hold no place, and none of probes, tail pieces, logos or cuts): a series decision's in its series and in every other series where it is one of the decisions whose agreement decides the unit, a global one's in the series `--series` names or in every series.

`junk redecide ROW… junk|keep [--series SERIES…] [--retire]` is 1 act, recorded with its time and printed with its id: it re-decides active decisions to the verdict named, in 1 transaction, refusing all, writing nothing, when it refuses one. A series decision is retired and a decision of that verdict written in its place. A global decision stays and gets series decisions of that verdict in each series `--series` names; or, with `--retire`, is retired, and its units become candidates again; or, with neither, a global junk decision gets keeps in every series whose sightings hold a unit it decides now and is retired, and a global keep is retired. A series decision with `--series` stays, and each series named gets a decision of that verdict on its kind and key (refused where that series already has an active decision on the key). A decision an act writes is a human's and keeps the images of the decision it replaces; it carries no chain (a chained decision re-decided `keep` is a plain keep, re-decided `junk` again a plain block; `junk undo` of the act brings the chained one back). It refuses: a cut decision (changed only by `junk undo` of the act that wrote it), an unknown decision, a decision or series named twice, an inactive decision, a verdict equal to the decision's, `--retire` on a series decision; `--series` on a series decision naming its own series or of a kind agreement never decides (piece, cut); 2 decisions named with `--series` on 1 kind and key; both together, a series that does not exist, a series that already has its own decision on that key, a global junk decision no sighting holds when neither option is named, and an act after whose writes a written junk key is decided the other way in its series (naming the deciding decision).

`junk undo ACT` reverses an act in 1 new act: the decisions it wrote are retired and the decisions it retired are active again, as they were; every change an act makes stays in its history, which `junk rows` shows. It refuses an unknown act, an act already undone, an undo, an act a decision of which a later act has since changed (naming it: undo that first), and an act whose retired decision's key now has another active decision.

`junk cut --spans ID` draws open block or probe candidate ID's unit (each match of its key) with 300 rows each side, in the lowest-id chapter waiting on it with a current strip scan and a usable download, names the nearest seed above and below (even outside the drawn rows), and prints that chapter's id (a waiting chapter with no content digest or an unreadable download is passed over); refused when no unit of that chapter matches the key; a piece, logo or page-layout candidate is refused (name ranges on its chapter's head or tail instead). `junk cut --spans CHAPTER head|tail` draws the chapter's first or last 6000 rows. `junk cut --spans CHAPTER A:B` draws rows A to B (at most 6000, clamped to the strip) of a strip chapter, every unit there matching an open block or probe candidate of its series marked by a green bar in the ruler with the candidate's id; it prints each marked unit's candidate and rows, and is refused for a window over 6000 rows, one past the strip's end, and a chapter of a series that is no strip. Each span is labelled with its rows as a range names them (`START:END`, END exclusive), seeds are marked, rows the chapter's decide already removes are shaded, and named `removed START:END` beside them, a ruler gives every 100th row, and each sheet is at most 1,568 px long and 1.15 MP. It prints the sheets' paths and writes nothing else.

`junk cut CHAPTER:A:B[:keep]… [--credit] [--inside]` proposes a cut decision: 1 version per named chapter (in the order named), rows A–B of that chapter's strip cut (or kept, `:keep`), each end snapped to a span boundary within 1 gutter (`--inside`: an end strictly inside a span stays: an exact boundary, whose piece is followed by the rest of its span), anchored at a seed its strip has as decided without it (the edges of the ranges removed before the join rule, gap and strip-end seeds, and each match's far edge, even one ending in a keep; both ends at one: the seed ranking first (removed, strip end, chained, gap), then the upper end; an anchor inside a span needs an exact first piece), checked to match in its chapter alone (a version that matches nowhere there is refused; the refusal names the named cut ranges already removed: at most 1 gutter of their rows outside the rows its decide without the cut removes). The named chapters, and the 200 most recent other chapters of the series (by chapter id) holding a current strip scan, are then decided with and without it. It prints each checked chapter's outcome (taken; miss: a miss the decide with it has and the decide without it lacks, or rows the decide without it removes that it keeps, any part longer than 1 gutter, named; no cut), 1 line counting the chapters not checked (not done before or at junk with no current scan, no usable download, or beyond the 200), the ranges it also removes, its sheets (each named range, and each range it also removes in any checked chapter), and, when no outcome is a miss, a digest of its inputs; it writes nothing else. It is refused when it names no cut range (only `:keep` ones), an end has no boundary near it, neither end lies at a seed, a piece is uniform or unsignable, a span between ranges is not named, ranges overlap, the named chapters are of 2 series, a named chapter has no content digest, its pages cannot be read, it has neither a usable download nor a retention original (a named chapter with no current scan is scanned again from its pages, OCR on, the scan stored nowhere) or is not a strip, its key span is another active cut decision's, or a changed chapter fails the kept check.

`junk cut --edge ID above|below` proposes a junk decision on open block or probe candidate ID carrying its card's side: an edge version from the lowest-id chapter waiting on ID (current strip scan, usable download) where the side removes rows, its refs that chapter's units of ID in the half toward that edge (below: 2 × the unit's end ≥ the strip's height; above: 2 × its start ≤ it); refused when no waiting chapter has one; checked as a cut is.

`--record DIGEST` with the same arguments records it in 1 act when the inputs digest is unchanged (else `stale`): the cut decision, or the candidate's junk decision (with the candidate's images) and its side; the decision is built again before the act's transaction, which checks the key span free (a cut) or the candidate open (a side), reads the digest again and writes. The digest covers the arguments and the decision built (its key, chain and credit), the ids of the series' and the global active decisions and the store's version (its largest decision id and largest act id), the series' watermark keys, every chapter of the series holding a current strip scan (id, content digest, scan time) whether or not its download is usable, and each named or picked chapter's id and content digest. It prints the act id; `junk undo` of the act removes the decision. The decider is `claude` under a review id, else `human`. Inside a review the sheets go to `<paths.candidates>/review-<id>-cut/` (removed with the review's other sheets); otherwise to `<paths.scratch>/junk-cut/<process id>/`, removed by a later `junk cut` once 1 day old; the pages a call reads are extracted under it and removed when it ends.

A recorded cut covers no candidate: a chapter waiting on a candidate the cut lies in is decided again (with the cut) once that candidate has its own verdict.

`junk late` (no lock) lists the published files whose junk edit would remove other pixels today. Its files are those `rewrite rebuild` takes but for the original (pipeline, confirmed, not joined, not busy, series ok and in scope) whose `junk_chapter` has a kept scan. For each it runs the junk decide on the kept scan with today's decisions, watermarks and cut decisions; a strip whose decide tries a chain or cut decision reads the pixels from the file's original. A file is late when the rows, pages, bands, joins or blanks removed, or its split or split page count, differ from its edit (a decision id alone is not a difference). Each listed file gives its id, path, the rows or pages removed or kept anew, the cause (decisions; watermarks learned since; both; not told when the decide with the kept watermark keys cannot read the original), and whether its original is still in retention. A file whose decide is now refused, whose series' layout changed since the scan, or whose original cannot be read is listed with why; a kept scan the decide cannot read stops the command, naming the file. Read only: it writes nothing and queues nothing; `rewrite rebuild` takes the ids. Exit 0; 1 on a kept scan that cannot be decided; 2 with no database; 4 on a failed preflight check.

`junk backup DIR` writes the junk knowledge (the decisions, history included, the acts and their log, the sightings and the watermarks), each table with its column list, from 1 snapshot, into a new folder `junk-<UTC time>` in DIR. `junk restore DIR` writes a backup back, ids kept, into a database whose junk tables are all empty and have those columns, building the database when none is; it runs under the "nothing else is running" lock exclusive, before any part has run on a new database (a part's baseline would fill the decisions).

### The chapter edit

A decided chapter's edit is 1 JSON object, read by convert. It always holds all 7 keys; a key that does not apply to the chapter's layout holds its empty value. Page indexes are integers: the download's pages in archive order (`archive.archive_pages`); a cover-named page is never in any of them.

| Key | Type | Empty | Layout | Holds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `drop_pages` | list of int | `[]` | pages | the pages dropped: junk pages and frames, white end pages |
| `bands` | list of `{"page": int, "box": [int, int, int, int]}` | `[]` | pages | a page cropped by its junk bands: `box` is the part kept, `[left, top, right, bottom]` (right and bottom exclusive) in the pixels of the page as decoded (`images.open_flat`) |
| `join` | list of `[int, int]` | `[]` | pages | `[page, sliver]`: a sliver is a page cut off the bottom of the page before it (same width, at most half its height, the seam continuous); rejoined under it. A page in 3 pieces is `[[a, b], [b, c]]` |
| `split` | bool | `false` | pages | true when the chapter is stitched (whole pages joined into tall images) or re-sliced (its page stack cut at another height): convert cuts it back into pages; a split chapter has no drops, bands or joins |
| `split_pages` | int or null | `null` | pages | a re-sliced chapter's page count: convert cuts its stack into that many equal pages; null for any other chapter |
| `blank` | list of `{"page": int, "box": [int, int, int, int], "fill": [int, int, int]}` | `[]` | both | a junk logo: `box` is its padded box, `[left, top, right, bottom]` (right and bottom exclusive) in the pixels of the source page before any crop, `fill` its ring's median colour (0–255); at most 1 per page, never on a dropped or cover-named page |
| `strip_removed` | list of `{"start": int, "end": int, "why": str, "decision"?: int, "hash"?: str}` | `[]` | strip | rows removed, `end` exclusive, in the stack of the non-cover pages at their modal width; final (the gutter rule and the minimum length applied). `why`: `match` (a junk decision on an edge block, probe or remainder), `piece` (a junk decision on a tail piece), `blank` or `wedge` (removed by rule after the credit start), `join` (blank rows the gutter rule removes), `card` (a chained block or probe decision's card beside its unit), `cut` (a cut decision). A range a junk decision removed (`match`, `piece`, `card`, `cut`) names it: `decision` (its id), and for `match` and `piece` `hash` (the removed unit's signature text); a range a rule removed names neither |

Convert fills blanks first, on the source pages (only for a chapter it encodes to AVIF), then drops pages, then crops bands, then joins; joins are found among the kept pages, cropped where cropped. Convert treats a chapter with no edit, or a malformed one, as `needs_human` (Phase 6's rule, SPEC "Chapter stages"). Malformed includes, beyond a wrong key or type: a page index the chapter lacks, a page dropped twice, a drop or a band on a cover-named page, 2 bands on 1 page, a band on a dropped page, a band `box` that is empty or not inside its page, a `strip_removed` entry with other keys than `start`, `end`, `why`, `decision` and `hash`, with a `decision` that is not an integer or a `hash` that is not a signature's text or stands without a `decision`, with rows that are not `0 <= start < end`, or ending past the strip's last row; a `split_pages` that is neither null nor an integer of 2 or more, or an integer while `split` is false.

When junk ends Done, the chapter's scan and the watermark keys its decide used are kept on its edit row, with the time; each export clears those older than `export.retention_days` (`state reset` deletes them with the edits). Only `junk late` reads them. A schema step that changes the scan's shape changes both `junk_scan` and the kept scans.
