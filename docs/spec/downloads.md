# manga_pipeline spec — Downloads and Suwayomi

Part of the spec; the index is [`SPEC.md`](../../SPEC.md). Update this file in the same change as the behaviour it describes.

## Bad downloads

A chapter whose download is bad (see "Validation") is downloaded again through Suwayomi, first from the same Suwayomi chapter once, then from other sources by rank. The rejected CBZ is removed the retention way (SPEC "Publishing"), then, when it has a Suwayomi chapter, deleted through Suwayomi, which clears its downloaded mark (the file being gone already is no error to Suwayomi). A hand-placed chapter (SPEC "Suwayomi") has no same chapter to try: it goes straight to other sources, and its original source, the reference for the numbering check below, is the Suwayomi manga linked to its series whose source ranks best in `sources.ranking` (then the lowest manga id); that manga's own source is not passed over. A series with no linked Suwayomi manga has nothing to check numbering against: the chapter becomes `needs_human` at once, no source tried. The rejection is recorded as a handled chapter (`rejected`) with the rejected file's digest, under the chapter's own identity. Nothing is added to Suwayomi's library: a source search stores its hits outside it, and their chapters can be downloaded from there.

`sources.ranking` lists source display names (`"<name> (<LANG>)"`), best first. The next source is the highest-ranked installed one not tried yet for the chapter and not the original's; an unranked source is never picked automatically. On it the series is searched by each of its Kavita names and the manga's title; the hits of all searches are pooled, and the series is the 1 hit whose title equals a known name under letters-and-digits lower-casing, else the 1 hit whose fuzzy ratio to a known name is at least 90 with every other hit at least 10 below; else the source is skipped. The chapter is the source's 1 chapter whose parsed season, side label and number equal the chapter's (more than 1: the one with the chapter's scanlator, else the source is skipped), and the source's numbering must agree with the original's: of the original source's chapters of the same season and side label numbered within 10 of the chapter's, at least 3, and at least 90 % of them listed on the other source under the same season, side label and number (with titles, where both have one, at a fuzzy ratio of at least 80); else the source is skipped. A Suwayomi chapter another open chapter holds (as its download, or one it expects) is never picked: its source is skipped. A skipped or failed source counts as tried. A source that cannot answer now is passed over without counting as tried; when every remaining source cannot answer, the chapter fails (1 attempt). When no source is left, the chapter becomes `needs_human`, naming the sources tried.

After queueing the download, the pipeline starts Suwayomi's downloader, and the chapter waits on `suwayomi_download` with the download it expects (Suwayomi chapter, source, the path Suwayomi will write, when it was queued). The pipeline records the expected download before it queues it, so a download Suwayomi makes is never untracked. `intake` ends the wait when the expected chapter is flagged downloaded, its queue entry is in error, it is neither queued nor downloaded (then it is queued again, and the wait goes on), or, while not downloaded, `sources.download_timeout_hours` have passed since it was queued; Suwayomi unreachable during this check ends `intake`'s run. Then validate looks at it in place, without going back to discover: a download flagged with its file present is validated as the chapter's new file, and only when it passes does the chapter take its path, Suwayomi chapter, source and digest; its identity never changes, whatever source, title or numbering it arrived under. A bad replacement is rejected like the first copy, and the next source is tried. A download in error, missing, flagged without its file, or timed out is removed from Suwayomi's queue, its file (if any) removed as above, and the next source is tried. A path an open chapter holds or expects is never taken as a new download nor listed as not taken.

A person settles a chapter that is `needs_human` at validate with `chapter use CHAPTER_ID SUWAYOMI_CHAPTER_ID` (that Suwayomi chapter is the replacement: the file in hand, if still there, is rejected, and the next `intake` queues the chosen one; a chosen chapter that does not exist, that another open chapter holds, or whose download is numbered otherwise (season, side label or number) leaves the chapter `needs_human`) or `chapter drop CHAPTER_ID` (also at discover or junk: the download, if still there, goes the retention way, then the chapter is done and recorded as a handled chapter, `dropped`, with its digest). Both take no lock and are refused with exit 2 unless the chapter is `needs_human` at those stages; `chapter drop` is also refused while a re-download still has a copy to remove or a download it expects (`retry` lets `intake` finish them). `retry` on a chapter being re-downloaded starts the source search again from the top (same source first) and forgets a person's choice; what it rejected stays recorded. A download numbered otherwise than its chapter is held at validate on every look, so a retry holds it again.

The re-download state is workflow state: `state reset` clears it. A replacement already queued then arrives as an ordinary download: a Suwayomi chapter's when its manga is in Suwayomi's library, else a hand-placed one of the folder it lies in.

### Validation

Validate reads every page of the download in full, in archive order, in this order of checks: each page's bytes and format, page by page; then every page decoded; then every page's orientation; then the series-wide checks. The first defect found decides. The page measurements (bytes, sha1, width, height, format, blank flag, pHash, junk signature, colour class, chroma) are stored for `process`; they describe the download's pages and are never changed afterwards.

| Found | Outcome |
|---|---|
| an archive that cannot be read (broken, truncated, CRC error, encrypted), no pages, a zero-byte page, a page of a known format that does not decode | bad download: re-downloaded (above) |
| a page of unknown format (by its first bytes), or JPEG XL | `needs_human` |
| a page with an EXIF orientation that turns or mirrors it (2 to 8) | `needs_human` |
| mislabelled: at least 90 % of its non-blank pages within pHash Hamming distance 6 of a non-blank page of the series' published files of other chapters (another season, side label or number, or another volume where the volume tells chapters apart: Library copies) | `needs_human` |
| the same page bytes twice | noted (`status`), kept |
| short: strip length (sum of height × 1000 / width) below 40 % of the median of the series' published files | noted (`status`), published |

The mislabel and short checks run once the series has at least 3 published files with stored page measurements (pHashes for the one, strip length for the other); a published file with none stored does not count. The pipeline stores them when it publishes a chapter it measured; the bulk converter's files have none. Junk members, folders, wrong extensions and page names are not defects: `process` writes the pages anew.

A download whose name, without its scanlator prefix and chapter number, is one of `validate.non_chapter_names` (a notice or hiatus page) and that has no chapter number (in its name or its `ComicInfo.xml` `Number`) is dropped at discover, removed the retention way and recorded as a handled chapter, `dropped`. A numbered one goes on as a chapter and is judged once at junk, after the library-copy check, by the bulk converter's rule: it is a chapter when its title has an end marker (`[END]`, `(Finale)`), when its page count is at least the series' median, or when no median is known; it then carries a note (`status` lists it), written at once, and a chapter carrying that note is not judged again. Otherwise it is dropped there the same way as a notice. The median is like with like and needs at least 3 files: in a series of layout `pages` over the source page count of its published files (a bulk-converter file's output page count where none is stored); in a series of layout `strip` over the source page count of the files the pipeline published (a bulk-converter strip file's pages are re-cut). A dropped chapter carries no notes. An archive that cannot be read is not judged at discover: the chapter takes its number from its name alone and goes on to validate.

### Library copies

A published file is of the chapter when it has the chapter's season, side label and number, and, for a main chapter, a volume that does not differ from the download's where both come from the files: the download's own (its name's, else its `ComicInfo.xml` `Volume`; a `ComicInfo.xml` that cannot be read gives none) and a published file's whose volume came from its file name; 2 such volumes are 2 chapters (so a series that starts its numbering again in each volume keeps them apart). A volume a source or rule assigned (MangaDex, an override file, seasons) never splits a chapter: a source may move a chapter to another volume later. The published file's volume is its `ComicInfo.xml` `Volume`, `01` and `1` alike. Only confirmed published files are compared here. A download volume that is not a whole number makes the chapter `needs_human` here, and at validate's mislabel check, which compares volumes the same way (with every published file, confirmed or not). A side story is told apart by season, side label and number alone: its volume is assigned.

When a chapter reaches junk (the series' baseline exists from validate) and the series already has a published file of the chapter, still in the library:

- 2 or more such files: `needs_human`.
- Not the same chapter: `needs_human`. The same chapter = its page count (series layout `pages`) or strip length (layout `strip`) within 15 % of the published copy's original (a cover page the published file holds not counted).
- A better copy replaces the published file (SPEC "Retroactive updates"): it is named as that file is, keeps its volume cover, and goes on as the file's 1 open chapter. A higher edition rating is better whatever else (judged at once); a lower one is never better. At the same rating the copy goes through the junk scan and decision first, then: better = neither its median page width nor its bits per pixel below 0.95 × the published copy's original, no more junk removed, and at least 1 of: width at least 1.05 ×, bits per pixel at least 1.2 ×, less junk removed. A width or bits per pixel below 0.95 × is a duplicate at once. Junk removed is the pages the junk decision drops (layout `pages`) or the strip rows it removes per 1000 px of page width, the gutter rule's rows left out (layout `strip`; more or less = by more than 500); the published copy's is what its own download lost (stored when the pipeline published it, else from the bulk converter's record of its edits). Bits per pixel are 8 × the pages' bytes over their pixels; a bulk-converted file's pages were re-encoded, so its bits per pixel are not compared, nor junk where it is not known. A file busy with a change (an open chapter on it, a queued rewrite, a wait for Kavita, a cover refresh due, or a failed rewrite) makes the copy `needs_human` naming it.
- Otherwise the download is a duplicate: dropped, removed the retention way and recorded as a handled chapter, `duplicate`.

A download at junk waits (`open_copy`) while another download of its series that is not done has its chapter key and is ahead of it: past junk (convert, comicinfo, publish or confirm); or at junk and naming a published file (a better copy that took its file) while this one names none; or at junk, both naming a published file or neither, and discovered before it (by discovery time, then id). A download at discover or validate is never waited on, and a rewrite chapter is not a download. When the wait ends the download is judged as any other: the copy that went first is done by then. Confirmed, the later one is a better copy or a duplicate of it; a better copy whose file's rewrite is still pending makes that file busy, and a later copy better than the file is held needs_human, to be retried once the rewrite has finished (above). 2 copies whose volumes tell them apart (above) wait the same way and are then both published. A copy ahead that is held `needs_human` holds the later one until a person settles it: by `chapter drop` at junk, or at any stage when it names a published file, otherwise by fixing its fault and `retry`.

The published copy's original is what the pipeline stored when it published that file. A published file with no stored original (the bulk converter's, or one whose chapter had no page measurements when it was published) is measured from the library file itself: its page count and its pages' widths and heights, read from each page's whole bytes without decoding it; its junk was removed, so it is shorter than its original. Edition ratings, from the download's name and a published file's source name (the bulk converter's source file, or the download the pipeline published): uncensored ([R18], [R19], [🔞]) above untagged above censored ([R12], [R15]).

A chapter's identity is its scanlator, season, side-story label, number and title key, parsed from the chapter's name (its `ComicInfo.xml` `Number` gives the number when the name has none); scanlator and title compare by letters and digits only, lower-cased. Suwayomi's own chapter number is never part of it: it is per source, and numbers restart per season in some series. A number whose decimal part ends in 0 without being all zeros (26.10, written 26-10 by some sources; not 26.0) has no single reading, 26.1 or the 10th part of 26: the download is `needs_human` at discover, unless its pages are a handled chapter's (below), and it is never taken for chapter 26.1, as a first download or as a replacement. The identity is settled at the discover stage and never changes, whatever source a replacement comes from; the chapter keeps its number beside it (`chapter.number`), which every download past discover has.

The pipeline records every chapter it has handled (published, rejected, duplicate or dropped) with the content digest of its download: the sha256 of the ordered list of its pages' sha1s, so a re-zipped copy with the same images has the same digest. A download whose digest equals a handled chapter's of the same series is the same pages again: it is put aside (moved to the retention folder, SPEC "Publishing") and not processed: the chapter is dropped, and no second handled chapter is recorded, since the digest already is. A handled chapter recorded without a digest (an archive that could not be read) matches nothing. This is checked at discover, and again at junk once the series' baseline exists. A chapter is recorded as handled when it is published, in the same transaction as the rename's record, so a crash or a `state reset` never publishes it twice.

## Suwayomi

- `paths.downloads` is Suwayomi's download root; downloads lie under its `mangas` folder.
- A download is any CBZ under `mangas/<folder>/<folder>/`, whoever put it there: Suwayomi, or a person who places an externally sourced series or chapter by hand (any 2 folder names; the second is read as the series' title). A file is taken once its modified time is at least `intake.settle_minutes` old by this machine's clock, so a file still being written (Suwayomi writes a CBZ under its final name while it is incomplete; a copy over the share does the same) is skipped without a word until it settles. Suwayomi's downloaded flag plays no part in taking a download (it still ends a re-download's wait: Bad downloads). A file that stalled mid-write and settled is a broken archive, which validate handles as a bad download.
- `intake` finds downloads by listing the folder and maps each manga folder to a manga of Suwayomi's library by rebuilding the source and title folder names. In a folder 1 library manga maps to, a file 1 chapter of that manga rebuilds to (from the chapter's scanlator and name) is that Suwayomi chapter's download; a file no chapter rebuilds to is a hand-placed chapter of the manga's series. A folder no library manga maps to is a hand-placed folder: its series is found by its folder (SPEC "Series identification"), and its files are hand-placed chapters. A hand-placed chapter has no Suwayomi chapter and no source. A file becomes a chapter only when no unfinished chapter has its path and it is still there once that is known (a confirm may move it away meanwhile). Listing the folder opens no CBZ; identifying a hand-placed folder may read its files' `ComicInfo.xml`.
- A download that cannot be processed is not taken and is listed by `status` with its reason and when it was first seen: a folder 2 or more library manga map to, a file 2 chapters rebuild to, or a series that is new (SPEC "Series identification"). A download of a series out of scope is not listed. A manga folder that cannot be listed (a name the share cannot show this machine, such as one holding `?` over Samba) is listed as not taken, whatever its series, and the rest of the downloads are processed; a folder above it that cannot be read, or the share gone while it is listed, ends the run.
- Suwayomi's own updater skips series it marks completed. When a completed series' metadata check is due, `intake` asks Suwayomi to refresh the chapter list of each manga linked to it, so an epilogue or side story is found and downloaded: a chapter the refresh added is queued for download, and Suwayomi's downloader started, only when no published file, unfinished chapter or handled chapter of the series has its chapter key (season, side label, number), and only 1 chapter per key, whichever manga lists it. Chapters that were already listed and never downloaded are left alone.

## Series identification

A Suwayomi manga with downloads is matched to a series in this order, and the match is stored:

1. the series a stored link names (a link made earlier by this order, or by `series link`);
2. a source id of the manga equal to one stored for a series;
3. exactly 1 series of the library `kavita.library_id` whose name, localized name or original name equals the manga's title under Kavita's name normalising.

A series found by step 3 that already has a row (another source's manga of the same series) is that row. A source id of step 2 held by 2 or more series is a double hit. 2 or more strict hits, or a near hit (a series with a name, localized name or original name that equals the title under a lenient key: Unicode NFKC, case-folded, letters and digits only; or whose lenient key has a fuzzy ratio of 90 or more to the title's, or of which 1 lenient key of 15 or more characters starts with the other) make the manga's series `needs_human`, naming the candidates; its chapters are held. A manga with no hit at all is a new series (below). A series whose files lie in no single folder (SPEC "Services") is `needs_human`.

A Suwayomi library manga with no downloads and no link is matched by the same order on each `intake` run and linked on a hit in scope, so its series' check reads its trackers and its chapter dates. No hit, or a near or double hit, writes nothing and holds nothing; `metadata audit` lists it.

A hand-placed folder (no library manga maps to it) is matched the same way, with the folder in the manga's place: by its stored link (keyed by its 2 folder names under `mangas`), else by its title folder's name as step 3's title, else, when that has not exactly 1 strict hit, by the `ComicInfo.xml` `Series` its files carry as step 3's title (only when every file that carries a non-empty one, and can be read, gives the same name). The first name with exactly 1 strict hit links the folder; without one, a near or double hit of either name makes the folder's series `needs_human`, naming the candidates, and no hit of either is a new series. A held or new folder series is in scope when either name is (`series.only`); a held one is named by its title folder. Step 2 does not apply: a folder has no source id. A folder's series row keeps its `ComicInfo.xml` `Series` beside its title folder's name when the 2 differ.

A new series is admitted before any of its chapters is taken. Its row (origin `new`, no folder, no Kavita id) is created and linked when its manga or folder is first seen, holding the names it arrived under; until it is admitted its downloads are listed as not taken ("new series, metadata not fetched yet"). Its first metadata check runs at the next `intake` that reaches its sources (SPEC "Metadata checks"); once a check has completed, every `intake` run tries to admit it: its name is the one a person gave (`series new --name`), else the AniList English title, else its manga's title, else its files' `ComicInfo.xml` `Series`, else its title folder's name. The name must be unique under Kavita's name normalising among every name of every series in the library, the names of the other new series Kavita does not hold yet, and the folder names under the library root; and it must leave a folder name once made safe (the characters `\ / : * ? " < > |` become `_`, trailing dots and spaces go); and none of its ids (SPEC "Source ids", a `human` id winning) may be one a series in the library or an admitted new series holds (a double hit). Else the series is `needs_human`, naming the clash. Admitted, the series has that name as its `ComicInfo.xml` `Series` and the folder `<library root>/<safe name>` (its rules key), both never changed, and its downloads are taken by the next discovery.

A new series held by a near or double hit or by its name is settled by `series link` (it is another series) or `series new SERIES_ID [--name NAME]` (it is a new series): the series is ready to be admitted again, under NAME when given, its check is made due at once, and the next `intake` checks and admits it (its ids compared again, SPEC "Source ids"; after `series new` for a series whose check has completed, its ids are not compared with other series' again, its name still is). `series new` is refused with exit 2 unless the series is new, not admitted and `needs_human`; `retry --series` is refused with exit 2 for such a series. A held new series' chapters are recorded and held as any held series'.

A person links a manga with `series link MANGA_ID KAVITA_SERIES_ID`, or a hand-placed folder with `series link --folder SOURCE_FOLDER/TITLE_FOLDER KAVITA_SERIES_ID` (the 2 folder names under `mangas`, spelt exactly as listed; refused unless that folder exists and no library manga maps to it): the Kavita series must be in `kavita.library_id` and have a folder. The series row holding that Kavita id, else the manga's (or folder's) held row (no Kavita id), else a new row gets the Kavita id, names and folder, and the manga (or folder) is linked to it. A held row of the manga or folder (no Kavita id) is merged into that row: its chapters, handled chapters, links and ids a person set move there (an id the target already holds is kept once; a different one is then a disagreement, SPEC "Source ids"; a chapter past junk goes back to junk, so the series' baseline and handled chapters apply) and it is deleted with what else it holds. A manga or folder of an admitted new series is refused. A manga or folder whose row is another Kavita series' row with chapters is refused. Run again, it refreshes a series' stored names and folder. `retry --series` then clears `needs_human`.

A series' rules key is the last part of the first folder it gets (`/` and `\` paths alike); a later folder (`series link`, a renamed folder) leaves it. Its junk decisions, sightings, candidates and watermarks (SPEC "Junk decisions") and its volume override file (SPEC "Publishing") are kept under it, so a renamed folder loses none of them; an override file written for a folder's new name is not read. 2 series whose first folders share a name share a rules key.

## Source review

A person finds out, step by step, whether a series in the library has a better source in Suwayomi, and moves it there, with the `source` commands. Each command prints what it found; none moves on by itself, and only `switch`, `replace` and `retire` change anything: Suwayomi's library and download queue, and the series' manga links. Nothing of a review is stored.

Any machine of the fleet runs the `source` commands, the Windows `process` machines as well as the Linux one. A machine that runs them needs its own `suwayomi.url`, `kavita.url` and `kavita.api_key` (machine-local keys, which a machine that runs only `process` may leave at the example's: SPEC "Config"); the service settings check refuses it otherwise (exit 4). Every path a command reads or writes is a stored path made local for that machine (library files, download folders: SPEC "Config") or a path Kavita reports mapped to a local one (SPEC "Services"). `switch`, `replace` and `retire` take `intake`'s run lock, a lock of the database, so they exit 3 on any machine while `intake` runs on any other (SPEC "Run lock").

A series is reviewed only while Kavita holds it: in `kavita.library_id`, in scope (`series.only`), with confirmed published files. Every command naming a series refuses another one with exit 2; a new series still in flight is refused as "not in Kavita yet: run once it is published", since the better-copy rule (Library copies), which does the replacing, works on published files. A bulk-converted series is reviewed too: its files are measured as Library copies measures a published copy with no stored original (page count and widths from the library file, junk from the series record), with no bits per pixel.

A published file's measures are those Library copies compares: its original's median page width, its original's bits per pixel (not known for a bulk-converted file) and the junk its download lost; with its edition rating. A series' combined measures are the mean of each over its latest 10 confirmed published files, each mean over the files that have it. A series crosses a trigger when its combined junk is above 500 strip rows per 1000 px (layout `strip`) or at least 1 dropped page (layout `pages`), its median page width is at most 800 px, or its bits per pixel are below 0.8.

The numbering check of the re-download (Bad downloads: neighbours of the same season and side label, listed on the other source) takes its reference as a list of chapters' identities and numbers. Here the reference is the series' confirmed published files, so a series with no linked Suwayomi manga, or whose linked manga's source is gone, can be searched and sampled, and a manga it switched to is never checked against itself.

The steps:

1. `source survey` reaches no service: every series in scope with its combined measures and the triggers it crosses, those crossing the most first. It reads the library files of bulk-converted series, whose measures are not stored, so over the share it can take long. A series with a file that cannot be read (not a CBZ, a page size it cannot read) is listed with that file's id, path and error in place of its measures, and the survey goes on; an unreachable share ends it.
2. `source measure SERIES_ID`: each confirmed published file of the series (its id, chapter key, source, measures and edition rating) and the combined measures; then each Suwayomi manga linked to the series, with its source and whether Suwayomi's library holds it (a Suwayomi read).
3. `source search SERIES_ID [--source NAME]...`: on each installed source of `sources.ranking` (or each source named, ranked or not), the series is searched by its Kavita names, the `ComicInfo.xml` `Series` of its files and its linked manga's titles, and the hit is picked as a re-download picks it (Bad downloads). Per source it prints the manga found, its chapter count, how many of the series' published chapter keys it lists, whether its numbering agrees with the reference on the files a sample would take, and whether Suwayomi's library holds it or a series links it; a named source not installed, or one that cannot answer, is printed as such. A manga Suwayomi's library holds, or one linked to any series, is read from Suwayomi's stored chapter list, never fetched from its source (a fetch would add a completed series' new chapter before its metadata check, which reads every linked manga, can see it as new); any other is fetched, and Suwayomi stores it outside its library. `source sample` reads a manga's chapters the same way.
4. `source sample SERIES_ID MANGA_ID [--chapters N]` (N 3 by default) judges the manga's copies of N chapters (below).
5. `source switch SERIES_ID MANGA_ID --yes` links the manga to the series and adds it to Suwayomi's library; it removes nothing. Refused with exit 2 unless the series is reviewable, the manga exists with an installed source, and no other series links it. Run again on a manga already linked and in the library, it changes nothing and says so. Suwayomi's updater then downloads the manga's new chapters (`autoDownloadNewChapters`); its existing chapters come only by `replace`. The series' other manga stay in Suwayomi's library: a new chapter arrives from each, and the better-copy rule keeps the better copy and drops the other as a duplicate.
6. `source replace SERIES_ID MANGA_ID (--file ID... | --all) --yes`: for each confirmed published file named (its id from `source measure`; `--all`: every one), the manga's matching chapter (picked as a re-download picks it, against the reference) is queued, then Suwayomi's downloader is started. Refused with exit 2 unless the manga is linked to the series and in Suwayomi's library, and for an id that is no published file of the series. A file not confirmed, a file with no match, a file busy with a change (Library copies), a chapter key an unfinished chapter of the series has, and a match Suwayomi has downloaded already with its file in the download folder (Suwayomi queues no second copy; that file arrives as a download) are skipped and printed. Each queued chapter arrives as an ordinary download, and the better-copy rule decides at junk: a better copy replaces its published file, reading progress carried (SPEC "Retroactive updates"); any other is dropped as a duplicate.
7. `source retire SERIES_ID MANGA_ID --yes` ends an old source: it prints the series' published chapter keys that no other linked manga lists, then removes the manga from Suwayomi's library and deletes its link to the series, so neither discovery (which maps only library manga) nor the metadata check (which reads every linked manga) touches it again. Refused with exit 2 unless the manga is linked to the series and Suwayomi still has it (one it no longer has is refused as such), and while the manga has a chapter in Suwayomi's download queue, a CBZ in its download folder, or a chapter an unfinished chapter of the series holds or expects (as its download or a re-download's). A download folder is named by the manga's installed source; a manga whose source is not installed (the old source's extension removed) has no folder to name, so a CBZ in the folder of its title under any source folder refuses it (conservative: another source's manga of the same title refuses it too). A download folder that cannot be listed because the share dropped ends the command with nothing changed; it is never read as empty.

### Samples

`source sample` judges a candidate manga on the series' latest N confirmed published files in a run (same season and side label) of at least 4 published files: the latest season's main run, else a side label's; a series with no such run is refused with exit 2, as is an N below 1. Each file's chapter on the manga is picked as a re-download picks it, against the reference; a file with no match is printed and left out.

Each chapter's pages are fetched through Suwayomi's reader API (SPEC "Services"), with no download (a chapter its source cannot serve is printed and left out): nothing is queued, nothing is marked downloaded, and nothing is written under the downloads folder, so discovery never sees a sample. They are written as a CBZ under `<paths.scratch>/source/<series id>-<manga id>/`, on the machine's local disk, on Windows as on Linux; `source sample` empties `<paths.scratch>/source/` when it starts (a killed run's files never stay) and deletes its folder when it ends, also after a failure.

Each sample is checked as validate checks a download (Validation): a defect is printed and the sample left out. Then the junk stage's scan and decision run on it, OCR on the machine's `junk.ocr_device` (the sample uses no image tool or scorer, but the fleet version check, which `source sample`, `switch`, `replace` and `retire` run as every part does, reads each image tool's and the scorer's version, so the machine needs them: SPEC "Preflight"), with the series' stored decisions, layout and page aspects, writing nothing: no candidate, no sighting, no layout or page aspect, no watermark.
